The short version

I like building useful things in boring industries.

Especially when the boring industry is actually important.

Insurance is full of complicated forms, old workflows, confused customers, overwhelmed agents, and tools that somehow make the day harder. That is the kind of mess I like working on.

My work is part agency building, part sales operations, part training, part technology experimentation, and part “there has to be a better way to do this.”

Most of my best ideas start as messy problems customers, agents, or business owners keep running into over and over again.

“The best systems feel obvious after someone finally builds them.”

That is usually the goal: take something confusing, turn it into a process, make it repeatable, then make it easier for the next person. In insurance, that can mean a better sales script, a cleaner form, a smarter training tool, or a customer experience that does not require a dictionary.

Field notes

Operating principles I keep coming back to.

Make the complicated part visible.

If nobody can explain the bottleneck, nobody can fix it. Draw the messy process first.

Train the human, then improve the system.

Bad process plus new tools is just faster confusion. Fix the workflow before adding more layers.

Plain language wins.

Customers do not need insurance theater. They need clarity, options, and next steps.

Useful beats impressive.

The best business tools are the ones people actually use when the day gets busy.
